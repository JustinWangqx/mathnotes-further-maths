## Store probabilities as coefficients

For a non-negative integer-valued variable $X$, the probability generating function is

$$
G_X(t)=E(t^X)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} P(X=k)t^k.
$$

The exponent records the outcome and its coefficient gives the probability. Substituting $t=1$ adds the probabilities, so $G_X(1)=1$. The constant term is $P(X=0)$. For the number of heads in three fair tosses, the probabilities are proportional to $1,3,3,1$, giving $(1+3t+3t^2+t^3)/8$.

## Recover moments by differentiating

In the first derivative the term $p_kt^k$ becomes $kp_kt^{k-1}$. At $t=1$ the powers disappear, leaving the weighted sum of outcomes. A second derivative gives the factorial moment $E[X(X-1)]$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(X)&=G_X'(1),\\
E(X^2)&=G_X''(1)+G_X'(1),\\
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=G_X''(1)+G_X'(1)-[G_X'(1)]^2.
\end{aligned}
$$

Use the derivatives when the corresponding moments exist.

## Build the standard distribution formulas

- For a uniform variable on $1,\ldots,n$, each coefficient is $1/n$. Summing $t+t^2+\cdots+t^n$ gives $t(1-t^n)/(n(1-t))$ for $t\ne1$, with value one at $t=1$.
- For $X\sim B(n,p)$, the binomial theorem collects the terms $\binom nk(1-p)^{n-k}(pt)^k$ into $(1-p+pt)^n$.
- For the number of trials to the first success, write $p(1-p)^{k-1}t^k$ for $k\ge1$. The sum is $pt/(1-(1-p)t)$. Counting failures instead gives a series beginning at $t^0$.
- For $X\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda)$, the terms are $e^{-\lambda}(\lambda t)^k/k!$. The exponential series gives $G_X(t)=e^{\lambda(t-1)}$.

## Group a stopping-time series into cycles

Jamil and Yao alternate independent attempts with success probabilities $1/4$ and $1/5$, starting with Jamil. Success on attempt one contributes $t/4$. Success on attempt two requires Jamil to fail first, so it contributes $(3/4)(1/5)t^2=3t^2/20$. After two failures the game restarts in the same order. Each complete failed round multiplies the contribution by $(3/5)t^2$.

$$
G_X(t)=
\left(\frac t4+\frac{3t^2}{20}\right)
\left(1+\frac35t^2+\left(\frac35t^2\right)^2+\cdots\right)
=\frac{5t+3t^2}{20-12t^2}.
$$

The value at $t=1$ is one, as expected for a game that eventually ends with probability one.

## Multiply for independent sums and substitute for rescaling

If $X$ and $Y$ are independent, $t^{X+Y}=t^Xt^Y$ and the expectations factor. Thus $G_{X+Y}(t)=G_X(t)G_Y(t)$. In the product, the coefficient of $t^k$ adds the probabilities of all independent pairs of outcomes that sum to $k$.

If $aX+b$ takes non-negative integer values, then $G_{aX+b}(t)=t^bG_X(t^a)$ wherever these expressions are defined. Doubling the same variable moves each coefficient from power $k$ to power $2k$. Adding two independent copies multiplies the PGF by itself. For example, if $X$ counts heads on one fair toss, $G_{2X}(t)=(1+t^2)/2$ while two independent tosses give $(1+t)^2/4$.
