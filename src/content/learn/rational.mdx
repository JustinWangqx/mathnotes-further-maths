## Begin with the reciprocal curve

The equation $y=1/x$ means $xy=1$. When $x$ is positive so is $y$, and when $x$ is negative so is $y$. Near zero the magnitude of $1/x$ grows without bound, while far from zero it becomes small. This gives the two branches and the asymptotes $x=0$ and $y=0$.

Replacing $x$ by $x-2$ moves the excluded input to $2$. More generally $y=k/(x-h)+v$ has vertical asymptote $x=h$ and horizontal asymptote $y=v$ when $k\ne0$.

## Use division to see the asymptotes

First identify the denominator zeros. Cancel common factors for algebra while carrying the original restrictions into the sketch. A cancelled factor can leave a hole. A denominator zero with an uncancelled pole gives a vertical asymptote.

For linear over linear, division gives a constant plus a shifted reciprocal. For equal degrees, the leading coefficient ratio gives the horizontal asymptote. For a numerator one degree higher, the quotient is a straight line. For example:

$$
\frac{x^2}{x+1}=x-1+\frac{1}{x+1}.
$$

The remainder term tends to zero as $|x|$ grows, so $y=x-1$ is the oblique asymptote.

## Find turning points and the range

Mark intercepts, use $y'=0$ to locate possible stationary points, and inspect signs on the intervals between poles. For the notes' example $y=1/((x-1)(x-2))$, the two outside branches are positive. The middle branch is negative and reaches $-4$ at $x=3/2$, where the product in the denominator is $-1/4$. Its range is $(-\infty,-4]\cup(0,\infty)$.

<Graph name="two-poles"/>

To check a range algebraically, fix a possible output $y$ and solve for $x$. When the resulting equation is quadratic, the discriminant must be nonnegative. Check any $y$ that makes the quadratic coefficient vanish separately, and discard any excluded $x$.

## Transform the sketch

For $y=|f(x)|$, reflect the negative pieces upwards. For $y=f(|x|)$, keep the right-hand half and reflect it across the vertical axis. For $y=1/f(x)$, zeros of $f$ can become poles, signs are preserved wherever defined, and points with $f(x)=\pm1$ keep their heights. For $y^2=f(x)$, sketch $y=\pm\sqrt{f(x)}$ over the inputs with $f(x)\ge0$.

## Read inequalities from the branches

Find the intersections by solving the corresponding equality. Split the real line at intersections and excluded inputs, then compare curve heights on each interval. For example, $|1/(x-2)|<1$ gives $|x-2|>1$. The graph lies between $y=-1$ and $y=1$ for $x<1$ or $x>3$. The endpoints are excluded because the comparison is strict.
