## Read probability as area

A density $f(x)$ is non-negative and the total area under it is one. Probability over an interval is $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$. A single point has zero width and therefore zero probability, so the choice of strict or inclusive endpoints gives the same result. A density can be taller than one if it is concentrated on a short interval.

For example, if $f(x)=kx$ on $1\le x\le5$ and zero elsewhere, its area is $k(5^2-1^2)/2=12k$. The total probability condition gives $k=1/12$.

## Accumulate area to build the CDF

The cumulative distribution function $F(x)=P(X\le x)$ is the area to the left of $x$. For the density above, inside the support integrate $t/12$ from $1$ to $x$. To the left of the support there is no accumulated area, and to the right all the area has accumulated.

$$
F(x)=
\begin{cases}
0 & x<1,\\
(x^2-1)/24 & 1\le x\le 5,\\
1 & x>5.
\end{cases}
$$

<Graph name="cdf"/>

This graph is non-decreasing, and the slope is the density wherever $F$ is differentiable. If a density changes formula at a breakpoint, carry the area already accumulated into the next piece. For example, let $f(x)=2(x+1)/29$ from $1$ to $4$ and $2/29$ from $4$ to $8$, with zero density elsewhere. The first piece accumulates $(x^2+2x-3)/29$. At $4$ this equals $21/29$. The second piece begins at that value, giving $21/29+2(x-4)/29=(2x+13)/29$.

## Use the CDF to answer probability questions

The area between $a$ and $b$ is $F(b)-F(a)$, even when the interval crosses a breakpoint. With the two-piece density, $P(2<X<5)=23/29-5/29=18/29$. For a cutoff exceeded by ten percent, solve $F(a)=0.9$. As $F(4)=21/29<0.9$, use the second piece. Solving $(2a+13)/29=0.9$ gives $a=6.55$.

A percentile is a horizontal location read from the CDF, while a mode is a location where the density peaks. To find the mode, compare candidates from stationary points, endpoints and breakpoints of the density.

## Moments are weighted areas

For a function $g$, $E[g(X)]=\int g(x)f(x)\,dx$ over the support. This is the continuous counterpart of summing values times probabilities. For the first density, $f(x)=x/12$ on $[1,5]$, the moments are

$$
\begin{aligned}
E(X)&=\int_1^5\frac{x^2}{12}\,dx=\frac{31}{9},\\
E(X^2)&=\int_1^5\frac{x^3}{12}\,dx=13,\\
\operatorname{Var}(X)&=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2=\frac{92}{81}.
\end{aligned}
$$

For $E(\sqrt X)$ the integrand would be $\sqrt x f(x)$, so the power changes before integration.

## Transform a variable by transforming the event

Find the support of $Y=g(X)$ first. Write $F_Y(y)=P(g(X)\le y)$, solve the inequality in terms of $X$, then differentiate the new CDF inside its support if a density is needed.

If $F_X(x)=x^2/16$ on $[0,4]$ and $Y=X^2$, the transformation is increasing there. For $0\le y\le16$,

$$
F_Y(y)=P(X\le\sqrt y)=F_X(\sqrt y)=\frac{y}{16}.
$$

That gives a uniform density $1/16$ on $[0,16]$. For a decreasing example, take $f_X(x)=8/(3x^3)$ on $[1,2]$ and let $Y=1/X$. Now $Y$ lies in $[1/2,1]$ and $1/X\le y$ means $X\ge1/y$. Integrating from $1/y$ to $2$ gives $F_Y(y)=(4y^2-1)/3$, and differentiation gives $f_Y(y)=8y/3$ on that interval.
