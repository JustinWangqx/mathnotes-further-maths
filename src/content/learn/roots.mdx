## Start with the factors

Let $ax^2+bx+c$ have roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Each root gives a factor. Expand the factors and compare coefficients of the same powers of $x$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
a(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)&=ax^2-a(\alpha+\beta)x+a\alpha\beta,\\
\alpha+\beta&=-\frac ba,\\
\alpha\beta&=\frac ca.
\end{aligned}
$$

The minus sign in the sum comes from selecting one negative root term. The product is positive in the expansion because two negative root terms multiply together. The same argument gives the alternating signs for higher degrees.

For $ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$, the sum of the roots is $-b/a$, the sum of pairwise products is $c/a$, and the triple product is $-d/a$. For a quartic the fourth elementary sum is the product of all four roots and is the constant divided by the leading coefficient.

## Reciprocal roots

With nonzero roots, $\alpha^{-1}+\beta^{-1}=(\alpha+\beta)/(\alpha\beta)$ and $\alpha^{-1}\beta^{-1}=1/(\alpha\beta)$. These can be used as the sum and product of the new roots. The same result comes from substituting $x=1/y$ and multiplying by $y^2$:

$$
a(1/y)^2+b(1/y)+c=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad cy^2+by+a=0.
$$

This explains why the coefficients reverse. The constant $c$ must be nonzero for the reciprocal construction.

## Power sums and identities

Write $S_k=\alpha^k+\beta^k+\cdots$. Squaring $S_1$ introduces two copies of every pairwise product. Subtract those copies to isolate $S_2$. For two roots, expanding the cube similarly gives $S_3=(\alpha+\beta)^3-3\alpha\beta(\alpha+\beta)$.

For the example $2x^2+3x-2=0$ in the notes, the sum is $-3/2$ and the product is $-1$. Thus

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_2&=(-3/2)^2-2(-1)=17/4,\\
S_3&=(-3/2)^3-3(-1)(-3/2)=-63/8.
\end{aligned}
$$

## Build a recurrence from the original equation

Every root satisfies its polynomial. For $x^3+2x^2+5=0$, multiply the equation for each root by its $k$th power and add:

$$
S_{k+3}+2S_{k+2}+5S_k=0.
$$

Here $S_0=3$ because there are three roots. Coefficient comparison gives $S_1=-2$ and $S_2=4$. Taking $k=0$ gives $S_3=-2(4)-5(3)=-23$. Since the constant is nonzero, negative powers are allowed. Taking $k=-1$ gives $S_2+2S_1+5S_{-1}=0$, hence $S_{-1}=0$.

## Change the roots by substitution

State the new root as a function of the old root, then solve that relation for the old variable. For example, to divide the roots of $x^2-2x+12=0$ by three, write $y=x/3$, so $x=3y$. The new equation is $9y^2-6y+12=0$, or $3y^2-2y+4=0$.

For a reciprocal shift $y=1/(x-2)$, use $x=2+1/y$ and clear the denominators. Check the original restrictions before accepting the transformed roots. The substitution acts on the variable in the whole equation, including each power.
