## Projections, perpendiculars and area

The dot product is $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta$. Dividing by $|\mathbf b|$ gives the signed component of $\mathbf a$ along $\mathbf b$. The cross product points perpendicular to both vectors, and its magnitude is $|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\sin\theta$. That is base times height for the parallelogram, so the triangle has half that area.

The scalar triple product $\mathbf a\cdot(\mathbf b\times\mathbf c)$ is the signed volume of the parallelepiped. It takes the perpendicular component of one edge and multiplies it by the opposite face area. Take the absolute value for volume, and divide by six for the tetrahedron with those three edges from a common vertex.

## Describe a line and a plane

A line is $\mathbf r=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf u$, where $\mathbf a$ is a position vector on the line and $\mathbf u$ is its direction. A plane has two independent directions, so $\mathbf r=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf u+\mu\mathbf v$. Its normal is $\mathbf n=\mathbf u\times\mathbf v$.

Because every displacement within the plane is perpendicular to $\mathbf n$, $\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf r-\mathbf a)=0$. For example, a plane through $(2,1,0)$ with normal $(1,2,3)$ has equation $x+2y+3z=4$. Substituting a line into that equation gives the parameter of its intersection. If the parameter cancels, the line is either contained in the plane or parallel to it, according to whether the remaining equation is true.

## Distances are perpendicular projections

For a point $P$ and plane through $A$, project $\overrightarrow{AP}$ onto the normal and take its magnitude:

$$
d=\frac{|\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf p-\mathbf a)|}{|\mathbf n|}.
$$

For a point and line, the perpendicular distance is the height of the parallelogram formed by $\overrightarrow{AP}$ and the line direction $\mathbf u$. Its value is $|(\mathbf p-\mathbf a)\times\mathbf u|/|\mathbf u|$. To obtain the *foot* as well, put it on the line and set the dot product of the displacement from $P$ and $\mathbf u$ to zero.

For nonparallel lines, the common normal is $\mathbf u\times\mathbf v$. Project the displacement between any points on the two lines onto this normal for their shortest distance. For parallel lines, use the point-to-line formula.

## Angles and intersections

The acute angle between planes is the acute angle between their normals. The angle between a line and plane is complementary to the angle between the line and the normal, which gives

$$
\sin\theta=\frac{|\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf n|}{|\mathbf u||\mathbf n|}.
$$

For two nonparallel planes, their line of intersection is perpendicular to both normals. Use $\mathbf n_1\times\mathbf n_2$ for its direction, then solve the two plane equations together to obtain one point. For $x+y=3$ and $y+z=4$, setting $y=0$ gives $(3,0,4)$. The cross product of $(1,1,0)$ and $(0,1,1)$ is $(1,-1,1)$, so the intersection is $(3,0,4)+\lambda(1,-1,1)$.
