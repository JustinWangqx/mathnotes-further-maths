## Direction and position

A line through position vector $\mathbf a$ with nonzero direction vector $\mathbf d$ can be written $\mathbf r=\mathbf a+\lambda\mathbf d$. A plane with nonzero normal $\mathbf n$ has equation $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=c$.

Distinguish a point’s position vector from a direction. To find the direction from $A$ to $B$, subtract: $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf b-\mathbf a$.

## Scalar and vector products

$$\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\cos\theta,$$
$$|\mathbf a\times\mathbf b|=|\mathbf a||\mathbf b|\sin\theta.$$

The scalar product is a number; the vector product is perpendicular to both vectors. The vector product changes sign if you reverse the order. Parallel nonzero vectors have zero cross product.

## Example 1: the acute angle between lines

Two lines have directions $\mathbf d=(1,1,0)$ and $\mathbf e=(1,0,1)$. Their dot product is $1$ and each modulus is $\sqrt2$. Thus

$$\cos\theta=\frac{|\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf e|}{|\mathbf d||\mathbf e|}=\frac12,$$

giving an acute angle of $60^\circ$. The absolute value selects the acute angle between the undirected lines.

## Planes and intersections

To find a plane through three non-collinear points, take the cross product of two independent directions in that plane to obtain a normal. To intersect a line with a plane, substitute the line into the plane equation and solve for its parameter.

For an acute angle between planes, use their normals. For the acute angle $\phi$ between a line and a plane,

$$\sin\phi=\frac{|\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf n|}{|\mathbf d||\mathbf n|}.$$

This is the complement of the angle between the direction and the normal.

## Example 2: point-to-plane distance

Find the distance from $P=(2,0,1)$ to $x+2y+2z=1$. The normal is $(1,2,2)$ with length $3$, so

$$D=\frac{|2+2(0)+2(1)-1|}{3}=1.$$

In general, the distance from $\mathbf p$ to $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=c$ is $|\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf p-c|/|\mathbf n|$.

## Shortest distance between lines

For nonparallel directions $\mathbf d,\mathbf e$, lines through $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$ have shortest separation

$$D=\frac{|(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\cdot(\mathbf d\times\mathbf e)|}{|\mathbf d\times\mathbf e|}.$$

For parallel lines, use $D=|(\mathbf b-\mathbf a)\times\mathbf d|/|\mathbf d|$ instead. Check parallelism first; do not divide by a zero cross product.
