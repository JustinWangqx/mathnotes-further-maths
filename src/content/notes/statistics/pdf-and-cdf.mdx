## The idea

For a continuous random variable, probability is represented by **area**, not the height of a graph. A probability density function (PDF) $f$ describes how probability is distributed along the number line. A cumulative distribution function (CDF) $F$ gives the probability accumulated up to a point.

## What makes a valid density?

A PDF must satisfy both conditions:

$$f(x)\ge0,\qquad \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)\,dx=1.$$

Then

$$P(a<X<b)=\int_a^b f(x)\,dx.$$

A density value can exceed $1$; the total area must be $1$. For a continuous variable, $P(X=x)=0$, so including or excluding an individual endpoint does not change an interval probability.

## From PDF to CDF

The definition is

$$F(x)=P(X\le x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}f(t)\,dt.$$

A CDF is nondecreasing, takes values in $[0,1]$, and tends to $0$ and $1$ at the two extremes. For a continuous distribution, it is continuous. Where differentiable, $F'(x)=f(x)$.

For piecewise densities, carry forward the probability from earlier intervals. Restarting the integral at zero in each piece would make you lose accumulated area.

## Example 1: normalise and accumulate

Let $f(x)=kx$ for $0\le x\le2$, and $f(x)=0$ otherwise. Find $k$, the CDF and the median.

**Normalise the area.**

$$1=\int_0^2kx\,dx=2k\quad\Longrightarrow\quad k=\frac12.$$

**Integrate to a variable upper limit.**

$$F(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x<0,\\x^2/4,&0\le x\le2,\\1,&x>2.\end{cases}$$

For instance, $P(1<X<2)=F(2)-F(1)=1-1/4=3/4$.

**Find the median.** The median $m$ satisfies $F(m)=1/2$, so $m^2/4=1/2$. Since $m\in[0,2]$,

$$\boxed{m=\sqrt2.}$$

## Moments and variance

For a function $g$ whose expectation exists,

$$E[g(X)]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(x)f(x)\,dx.$$

In particular,

$$E(X)=\int xf(x)\,dx,\qquad E(X^2)=\int x^2f(x)\,dx,$$
$$\operatorname{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2.$$

The integration limits cover the full support, including every piece of a piecewise density. For the density in Example 1, $E(X)=4/3$, $E(X^2)=2$, and the variance is $2/9$.

## Example 2: transform using a CDF

Suppose $f_X(x)=2x$ for $0\le x\le1$, and $Y=X^2$. Find the distribution of $Y$.

**Step 1. Determine the support.** Squaring maps $[0,1]$ to $[0,1]$.

**Step 2. Translate the event.** For $0<y<1$, use $X\ge0$:

$$F_Y(y)=P(X^2\le y)=P(X\le\sqrt y).$$

**Step 3. Use the original CDF.** Since $F_X(x)=x^2$ on $[0,1]$,

$$F_Y(y)=F_X(\sqrt y)=y.$$

Thus $F_Y(y)=0$ for $y\le0$, $y$ for $0<y<1$, and $1$ for $y\ge1$. Differentiating gives density $1$ on $(0,1)$ and zero elsewhere, so $Y$ is uniform on $[0,1]$.

## Piecewise distributions: a checklist

1. Write the support before integrating.
2. Check nonnegativity and total area.
3. Build the CDF from left to right, preserving the accumulated area.
4. Check continuity at each boundary and the final value $1$.
5. For a quantile, find which piece contains the target probability before solving.

## Common mistakes

- Treating $f(x)$ as $P(X=x)$.
- Forgetting the CDF pieces outside the support.
- Integrating $f(x)$ instead of $xf(x)$ for the mean.
- Confusing $E(X^2)$ with $[E(X)]^2$.
- Transforming a variable without checking its support or whether the transformation is monotone.

For a non-monotone transformation, split the event into the relevant intervals. The simple square-root step in Example 2 works because $X$ is nonnegative.
